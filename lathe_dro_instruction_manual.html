01 / Electrical safety and tach sensor

Identify the sensor interface first

Power down before wiring. Stop the spindle, isolate machine power as appropriate, unplug the Pico, prevent accidental movement, and keep wiring clear of rotating parts, full axis travel, chips, coolant, and mains/VFD wiring.
  • Every Pico GPIO input must stay between 0 V and 3.3 V. 5 V, 12 V, 24 V, or negative-going signals can damage the Pico.
  • Scale inputs GP2-GP5 have no firmware pull-up/down. The Ditron scales default to TTL but may be ordered as EIA-422; confirm the output before choosing its interface. Never connect differential A-/B- directly to GPIO.
  • GP6 has an internal pull-up to 3.3 V and detects falling edges. It is not a 5 V level shifter.
  • The selected LJ12A3-4-Z/BX is a three-wire NPN normally-open inductive sensor rated 6-36 V; the listing gives brown = supply positive, blue = 0 V, black = output. Power it from a separate 12 V rail, not the Pico.
  • For direct GP6 use, confirm the black lead is an open-collector/sinking output with no pull-up to the sensor supply. Connect blue/0 V to Pico GND for this non-isolated interface; the GPIO then sees only the Pico's internal 3.3 V pull-up. If output topology is unclear, add a suitable interface and verify its Pico-side level.
  • Do not identify wires by color alone on other sensors. For non-isolated interfaces, connect a common signal reference only as specified. Do not assume the lathe frame or cable shield is signal ground.

LJ12A3-4-Z/BX tach pickup

  1. Power the probe from a regulated 6-36 V supply. The listed PC-PSU 12 V rail is within range; fuse and insulate the supply wiring.
  2. Mount a secure iron target at the probe's 4 mm nominal sensing distance. Do not use a loose target near rotating machinery.
  3. With the probe disconnected from GP6, verify the black output switches low when metal is detected and is not pulled up to 5/12 V. Confirm the open-collector/sinking output from the sensor datasheet or by a safe measurement.
  4. For a verified sinking output, connect black to GP6 and blue to Pico GND; leave GP6's internal 3.3 V pull-up enabled. Never connect brown or sensor supply voltage to a Pico pin.
  5. Firmware assumes four falling edges per revolution. Use four targets or change the firmware pulse count to match your target arrangement. Validate RPM against a trusted tachometer with guards in place.
Safe first power-up: Flash firmware with all sensor signals disconnected. Verify the USB link, then add one correctly interfaced signal at a time with power removed between wiring changes.

Assembly and routing

  • Use insulated connectors and strain relief suitable for machine vibration, chips, and coolant.
  • Route each signal pair with its return or as the interface specifies. Separate low-level signals from VFD, motor, contactor, and mains wiring.
  • Secure cables and check full carriage/cross-slide travel with power isolated so nothing pulls, pinches, or fouls.
  • If an axis direction is reversed, power down and swap that axis's A and B logic outputs at the interface, then retest.
  • Verify one falling tach edge per intended target. Do not add arbitrary capacitors or pull-ups; they may distort pulses or conflict with the interface.
03 / Firmware installation

Flash and start the Pico 2

Flash the supplied UF2 on Windows

  1. Stop the DRO on the Dell. Unplug the Pico from the Dell and use a USB data cable on Windows.
  2. Hold BOOTSEL while plugging the Pico into the PC. Release when detected. Device Manager may show RP2350 Boot; the boot volume commonly appears as RPI-RP2.
  3. Copy build-pico2/lathe_dro_pico.uf2 from the project folder onto the Pico boot volume. Do not format or initialize it.
  4. Wait for the volume to disappear as the Pico restarts. Reconnect it to the Dell normally, without BOOTSEL.

Optional firmware rebuild on Ubuntu

The project uses CMake, Ninja, ARM GCC, and Pico SDK 2.3.1.

sudo apt update sudo apt install cmake ninja-build gcc-arm-none-eabi git python3 git clone --branch 2.3.1 --recurse-submodules \ https://github.com/raspberrypi/pico-sdk.git pico-sdk git -C pico-sdk submodule update --init --recursive chmod +x build_pico2.sh ./build_pico2.sh

Output: build-pico2/lathe_dro_pico.uf2. Building does not flash the Pico; use BOOTSEL.

Dell Ubuntu application

A graphical desktop/session is required for Pygame; headless SSH alone cannot display the app.

sudo apt install python3 python3-venv python3-pip cd /path/to/lathe-dro-project python3 -m venv .venv .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt sudo usermod -aG dialout "$USER" # Log out/in after changing group membership chmod +x run_dro.sh ./run_dro.sh

The app auto-detects a Pico USB serial port. If necessary, specify the actual port, e.g. ./run_dro.sh --port /dev/ttyACM0. To stop a foreground DRO, press Ctrl+C in its terminal. Restart after reconnecting/reflashing.

Optional demo: ./run_dro.sh --demo --windowed. Simulated readings are not for calibration or machining.

Serial format: firmware sends lines like X:123,Z:-456,RPM:300 at about 60 updates/second. Raw counts restart at zero after Pico reboot; establish the work reference again.
04 / Calibration and controls

Verify before machining

X and Z scale calibration

The app defaults to 25,400 decoded counts per inch. Scale vendors may define cycles differently; SETUP calibration measures an effective scale factor but does not prove the scale's stated accuracy.

  1. Stop the spindle. Select INCH/MM on the main screen before entering calibration distance.
  2. Measure a long known straight-axis travel with a trusted standard. Approach the start consistently and account for backlash.
  3. In SETUP, select the X or Z distance field, enter the known travel, position at the start, and click CAPTURE X START or CAPTURE Z START.
  4. Move that axis by the entered distance, then click CALIBRATE X or CALIBRATE Z. The factor uses raw count change, independent of zero and display modes.
  5. For X, enter actual cross-slide (radial) movement, not twice that amount from the DIA display. Recheck over a second distance and in both directions when safe.
  6. Use the +/- 0.0001 buttons for fine adjustment only after checking mounting, missed counts, backlash, and signal integrity.
Persistence: calibrated X/Z factors and fine adjustments are saved in scale_calibration.json beside the app. Factors correct the base 25,400-counts/in assumption; verify against a trusted standard before machining.

Boring estimate

Enter the measured starting-hole diameter, position the tool at the starting bore wall, and capture X. The app adds twice the calibrated radial X travel. Choose X direction so the estimate increases while boring outward. Verify final diameter with a bore gauge.

RPM verification

Firmware measures falling-edge intervals and currently assumes four pulses/revolution. With the LJ12A3-4-Z/BX NPN NO sensor, use four secure iron targets at equal spacing or change the firmware pulse count. Confirm the sensor's switching rate is adequate at intended spindle speed and compare against a trusted tachometer with guards in place. RPM is advisory and does not control the spindle.

05 / Troubleshooting and sign-off

Commissioning checks

SymptomChecks
Pico not foundReconnect in normal mode; use a data USB cable; check Linux serial permissions/device path; restart DRO after flashing.
Scale does not countPower down. Check device/interface supply, safe logic level, signal reference, A/B wiring, connectors, and GP2/3 or GP4/5.
Axis direction reversedPower off and swap that axis's A/B interface outputs; retest known travel.
Erratic countsCheck receiver type, voltage, return path, loose terminals, electrical noise/cable routing, shield termination, and scale alignment. Do not guess pull-ups/capacitors.
RPM stays zeroCheck the sensor's 6-36 V supply, iron target/gap, verified NPN sinking output, GP6 wiring, and secure target mounting.
RPM wrong by ratioCount falling edges per revolution; firmware assumes four. Ensure the probe can switch fast enough.
Readings driftCompare several distances; check calibration, missed counts, backlash, scale mounting, and mechanical alignment.

Before first use

  • Exact scale and LJ12A3-4-Z/BX sensor part numbers/output specifications recorded.
  • Required scale interface installed; tach output confirmed NPN sinking/open-collector.
  • All Pico GPIO signals verified between 0 and 3.3 V.
  • Sensor supply/ground/signal wiring checked; no sensor supply voltage reaches a GPIO.
  • Wiring insulated, secured, labeled, and clear through full travel.
  • X/Z direction and travel checked against trusted standards in multiple positions.
  • RPM checked against a trusted tachometer with guards in place.
  • Operator confirms ABS/INC, RAD/DIA, unit, and tool selection before measuring.
Stop using the DRO if readings jump, reverse unexpectedly, disagree with trusted measurements, or wiring is damaged. Isolate power and correct the cause before machining. This system is not a safety interlock or motion controller.
